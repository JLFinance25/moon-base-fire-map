1. The problem

A Moon base will likely hold air with 30% oxygen or more at 10.2 psi or less (challenge page). More oxygen makes materials much more flammable.

NASA has run fire tests on the space station for years. Which of them ran in air like that?

Who it helps: students and the public, and mission designers, who can see where planned Moon base conditions go beyond what past space fire tests covered.

2. What we built

One dot = one ISS droplet-burning test (FLEX). Yellow = planned Moon-base air. Orange = NASA outcome "Disruption".

3. The NASA data

4. One honest result

Compare: tests below 30% oxygen ended in "Disruption".

Not a like-for-like comparison: none of the 24 tests at 30% oxygen or more added the table's "CO" gas (likely carbon dioxide, UNVERIFIED) or helium, which 122 and 50 of the other 249 tests did. Fuels and setups also differ. This does not show that oxygen caused "Disruption".

"Disruption" is NASA's label; what it means physically is UNVERIFIED. Counts recompute from scripts/analyze.py.

5. What is finished and what is not

  • Finished: chart, counts, sources, checked against 3 raw rows.
  • Limits: small liquid droplets, not a cabin fire; only 24 tests at 30% oxygen or more, all methanol or heptane droplets; FLEX only; not an interactive dashboard across many experiments.
  • Not done: other ISS fire experiments, what "Disruption" means.
  • AI use: an AI assistant (Claude) wrote most of the code and ran the data checks. I chose the idea, directed the work and reviewed the results.
